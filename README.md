# Solid Waste Management & Urban Flood Risk in Lagos, Nigeria

## Project Overview

This project investigates how solid waste management conditions may contribute to urban flood vulnerability in Lagos, Nigeria.

Lagos generates large volumes of municipal solid waste, while inadequate collection and improper waste disposal can contribute to drainage blockage. When drainage systems are blocked, the impacts of intense rainfall can become more severe.

The analysis combines waste-management data with historical evidence on drainage blockage and flooding to examine the relationship between solid waste, drainage conditions and urban flood risk.

> **Important:** This project investigates association and contribution, not direct causation. Waste management is only one factor influencing urban flooding alongside rainfall, drainage infrastructure, land use, topography and other environmental conditions.

---

## Research Question

**How does poor solid-waste management contribute to urban flood risk in Lagos, Nigeria?**

---

## Objectives

- Examine municipal solid waste generation and collection patterns.
- Measure the amount of waste that remained uncollected.
- Identify areas with recorded solid-waste-related drainage blockage.
- Examine historical evidence linking rainfall, waste blockage and flooding.
- Communicate the findings through an interactive Power BI dashboard.
- Demonstrate how environmental data can support evidence-based decision-making.

## Data Sources

### UN-Habitat Waste Wise Cities – Lagos WaCT 2021

The Waste Wise Cities Tool provided 2021 municipal solid waste indicators for Lagos.

Key figures include:

- Total municipal solid waste generated: **11,349 tonnes/day**
- MSW collected: **5,445 tonnes/day**
- MSW uncollected: **5,904 tonnes/day**
- Collection rate: **48%**
- Uncollected rate: **52%**
- Waste recovered/managed in controlled facilities: **887 tonnes/day**
- City recovery rate: **8%**

### Lagos Municipal Solid Waste and Flooding Study

Historical research on municipal solid waste and flooding in Lagos provided evidence on drainage blockage and flood vulnerability.

Key historical findings include:

- **222** recorded municipal solid-waste-related drainage blockage points.
- **126** streets affected by excessive rainfall and MSW-related drainage blockage.
- The five most affected LGAs accounted for approximately **50.9%** of recorded blockage points.

The historical figures are from the study period and should not be interpreted as current conditions.

## Key Findings

### 1. Large volume of waste remained uncollected

In 2021, Lagos generated approximately **11,349 tonnes of municipal solid waste per day**.

Of this:

- **5,445 tonnes/day (48%)** were collected.
- **5,904 tonnes/day (52%)** remained uncollected.

This indicates a substantial waste-management gap.

### 2. Drainage blockage was concentrated in several LGAs

Historical records identified **222 MSW-related drainage blockage points**.

The five LGAs with the highest recorded blockage points were:

| LGA | Blockage Points |
|---|---:|
| Shomolu | 36 |
| Lagos Island | 20 |
| Apapa | 19 |
| Lagos Mainland | 19 |
| Surulere | 19 |

Together, these five LGAs represented approximately **50.9%** of the recorded blockage points.

### 3. Waste management and flooding are interconnected

The findings indicate an association between waste-management conditions, drainage blockage and urban flood vulnerability.

However, waste alone does not explain flooding. Other important factors include rainfall intensity, drainage capacity and maintenance, land use, topography and urban development.

## Limitations

- The datasets cover different periods, so direct year-to-year comparisons should be interpreted carefully.
- Some drainage blockage and flooding evidence is historical rather than current.
- Waste management is only one factor influencing urban flooding.
- Historical blockage records may not represent all drainage blockage locations in Lagos.
- The UN-Habitat WaCT data is based on a rapid assessment and should be interpreted within its stated methodological limitations.

---

## Conclusion

The analysis shows that Lagos faces a significant municipal solid-waste management challenge, with approximately **52% of municipal solid waste remaining uncollected in the 2021 WaCT assessment**.

Historical evidence also shows substantial solid-waste-related drainage blockage across several Lagos LGAs.

Together, these findings demonstrate why improved waste collection, responsible waste disposal, regular drainage maintenance and better urban environmental management are important components of reducing flood vulnerability.

The project demonstrates how data analytics and visualization can be applied to real environmental and urban-development challenges in Nigeria.
